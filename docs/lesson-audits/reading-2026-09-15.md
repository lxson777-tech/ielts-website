Every claim below was checked against these, in this order of authority:

1. ielts.org, IELTS Academic Reading test format (the official test-format page). Source for the

   that matching-information answers are scattered rather than sequential.
3. The site's own real published tests, src/data/tests/reading-full-001.ts onwards (reused with
   publisher permission, recorded in each file's source block). I surveyed every instruction line
   in those files. They corroborate the official wording, and I quoted two of them into the

real reading tests):

## Reading Overview (reading-task1.html)

Checked and correct, left alone: 60 minutes; no extra transfer time; 40 questions across 3
passages; 1 mark each; 2,150 to 2,750 words; no negative marking; a misspelled answer is marked
wrong; every answer is in the text; the score-to-band table (it matches the conversion IELTS
publishes, and the page already carries the honest "approximate, boundaries can shift" caveat);
"Band 7 needs 30 of 40".

### 1. General Training wording left over in an Academic-only site (worst find on this page)

Before:
> Passage 2. Medium. Discursive text, often work- or training-related, with more paraphrasing.

"Work- or training-related" describes General Training Section 2, which this site no longer
covers at all. After:
> Passage 2. Medium. Often more analytical or discursive, on a topic of general academic
> interest, with heavier paraphrasing between question and text.

### 2. Difficulty ordering stated as a rule

Before: "Passages increase in difficulty from Passage 1 to Passage 3" and "Passage 1. Easiest." /
"Passage 3. Hardest." ielts.org does not state this; it says texts "range from the descriptive and
factual to the discursive and argumentative" and that "at least one text contains detailed logical
argument". Softened to "generally increase in difficulty", "Usually the most straightforward",
"Usually the hardest", and the official logical-argument fact was added in place of the invented
certainty.

### 3. 20 minutes per passage presented as a rule

Before: "Budget roughly 20 minutes per passage, then move on."
After, quoting what the real papers in this repo actually print:
> Budget roughly 20 minutes per passage, then move on. The paper itself prints "You should spend
> about 20 minutes on this passage and its questions", but that is advice, not a rule: the 60
> minutes covers all three passages together and nothing stops you moving between them.

### 4. "Answers appear in text order for most question types"

True of most types, but flatly misleading for the ones where it matters most. Replaced with:
> Most question types follow the order of the passage. Matching types and summary completion may not

### 5. Three gaps filled

- Text sources were listed as "books, journals, magazines and newspapers". Official wording adds
  "and online resources, written for a non-specialist audience", plus the descriptive-to-
  argumentative range. Added.
- The word-counting rules appeared nowhere on the overview. Added: a hyphenated word such as
  "check-in" counts as one word, a number counts as one word, and contracted words such as
  "they're" are never tested.
- The transfer-time pill said "60 minutes includes writing answers on the answer sheet". True on
  paper only, so it now says so and notes that on computer you type answers straight in.

## Multiple Choice (reading-mc.html)

### 1. Wrong number of options (the most serious error in the audit)

Before:
> You are given a question or an incomplete sentence with 3 answer options (A, B, C). You must
> locate the information in the passage and choose the single correct option.

Academic Reading multiple choice has four options. ielts.org: "a question with four possible
answers or the first half of a sentence with four possible sentence endings", and it also has
multi-answer variants. The site's own real tests use "Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D"
18 times and "Choose two letters, A-E" twice; none use three options. After:
> You are given a question, or the first half of a sentence, with four answer options
> (A, B, C, D). You must locate the information in the passage and choose the correct option.
> Some versions give a longer list of options and ask for more than one answer, for example two
> answers from five options (A-E) or three from seven (A-G); the instructions always say how many
> to choose, and each correct answer is worth one mark.

Three knock-on fixes in the same lesson:
- "Read the question and all three options" to "all of the options".
- "You may see information about all three options in the passage. Only one is correct." to
  "information about several of the options ... Unless the instructions ask for more than one
  answer, only one is correct."
- Trap pill "all options may appear in the passage. Only one is accurate" to "every option may
  appear somewhere in the passage. Appearing is not the same as answering the question".

### 2. "Form B" described imprecisely

Before: "Form B: A sentence to complete." That is the wording of a completion task. Official
wording is "the first half of a sentence with four possible sentence endings", so Form B now says
that, and a line was added distinguishing it from Matching Sentence Endings (where one lettered
list of endings is shared across several beginnings). The two were previously easy to confuse.

### 3. Real example added

The lesson had a passage but no worked question at all. Added, credited:
> A real one, to show the format. From Academic Reading Test 7, Question 38. The instruction reads
> "Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D." and the question is "What does the writer suggest
> about ABS in the fifth paragraph?" The four options are: A It is bound to make key decisions
> that are wrong. B It may reduce some of the appeal of the game. C It will lead to the
> disappearance of human umpires. D It may increase calls for the rules of baseball to be changed.
> The answer is B, and what you write on the answer sheet is the single letter B.

(Source: src/data/tests/reading-full-007.ts, question q38, answer "B".)

## True / False / Not Given (reading-tfng.html)

Checked and correct: the TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN definitions, that NOT GIVEN means the passage is
silent rather than that the statement is wrong, and that answers appear in passage order.

Two gaps filled rather than errors corrected:

1. The lesson never said the task is about facts, which is the whole distinction from
   Yes / No / Not Given. Added the official rubric ("Do the following statements agree with the
   information given in Reading Passage 1?") and an explicit line that this task is always about
   factual information, never the writer's opinions.
2. Added: "Write TRUE, FALSE or NOT GIVEN. Writing YES or NO on this task is marked wrong, even if
   the thinking behind it was right", and an explicit "Answers follow the order of the passage"
   bullet (it was only buried in a strategy step before).

## Yes / No / Not Given (reading-ynng.html)

Checked and correct: the whole facts-versus-opinions contrast, the NOT GIVEN definition, the
"quoted from someone else is not the writer's own view" warning, and the note that a statement can
be true in the real world and still be NO.

### 1. Unverifiable claim about which passage it appears on

Before:
> This task appears only with passages that argue a point of view, usually Passage 3, never with
> purely factual text.

"Usually Passage 3" is not stated anywhere official. After:
> This task only appears with a passage that argues a point of view. Official material says at
> least one of the three passages contains a detailed logical argument, and this task is set on
> that kind of text, never on a purely factual one.

### 2. Two rubric variants and the answer wording

The real tests in this repo use both "the claims of the writer" and "the views of the writer".
Added a note that they are the same task, plus "Write YES, NO or NOT GIVEN. Writing TRUE or FALSE
on this task is marked wrong."

## Matching Headings (reading-headings.html)

Checked and correct: more headings than paragraphs; a heading is a title for the whole paragraph,
not a matching detail; the trap about similar-sounding headings.

1. Missing official rule. ielts.org: "One or more paragraphs or sections may already be matched
   with a heading as an example on the question paper." This was absent, and it matters because
   students waste time on the used-up heading. Added:
   > One or more paragraphs may already be matched with a heading as an example printed on the
   > question paper. That heading is then used up and cannot be the answer to any of the numbered
   > questions, so cross it off before you start.
2. Internally contradictory example. The lesson said headings are "roman numerals: i, ii, iii" and
   then "Your answer must be a roman numeral (e.g. III)." Changed to "written the way it appears
   in the list (e.g. iii)".
3. "more headings than paragraphs" now reads "more headings than paragraphs or sections", since
   official material labels them either way.

## Matching Information (reading-matching-information.html)

### 1. Missing the defining property of this task type

The lesson never mentioned that this is one of the few types whose answers are not in passage
order. ielts.org describes the answers as not necessarily in order; IDP is blunter: "answers are
found in a mixed order in the article." Added:
> Unlike most reading tasks, the answers do not follow the order of the passage: question 2 can
> point to a paragraph that sits earlier in the text than question 1's.

Plus a new trap pill: "expecting the answers in passage order. This is one of the types that does
not follow it".

### 2. Reuse stated unconditionally

Before: "One paragraph can be the answer to more than one question". Official: only when the
instructions say so. The repo's real tests bear this out, with two rubrics carrying
"NB You may use any letter more than once" and two otherwise identical rubrics without it. After:
> A paragraph can be the answer to more than one question, but only when the instructions say so
> ("You may use any letter more than once")

and a new bullet, "Some paragraphs may not be used at all". The related trap pill was qualified
the same way.

### 3. Wording

"which labelled paragraph (A, B, C...)" now reads "paragraph or section", matching official
wording.

## Sentence Completion (reading-sentence.html)

Checked and correct: words must come from the passage; spelling must be correct; answers follow
passage order; the completed sentence must be grammatical.

1. Word limit wrongly generalised. Before: stay within the given word limit (usually "no more than
   three words and/or a number"). The limit varies per task and is printed with it. Across the
   repo's real tests, "ONE WORD ONLY" is by far the most common, not three words. After:
   > stay within the word limit printed with the task. The limit changes from task to task, for
   > example "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" or "ONE WORD ONLY", so read it every time.
2. Word-counting rules missing. Added the two official ones: a hyphenated word such as "check-in"
   counts as one word and a number counts as one word; contracted words such as "they're" are
   never tested.
3. Typo fixed in the same bullet list: "4 Words when limit is 3" to "4 words when the limit is 3".

## Summary, Note, Table & Flow-chart Completion (reading-summary-completion.html)

Checked and correct: the four layouts are one family of task; the two variants; the traps.

### 1. Ordering claim was wrong

Before: "Answers follow the order of the passage".

ielts.org states the opposite for this task type: "The answers may not come in the same order as
in the text. However, they will usually come from one part of the text rather than the whole
text." This is the one place where a lesson told students something the official page directly
contradicts. After:
> The answers may not come in the same order as the passage, but they usually all come from one
> part of it rather than the whole text

The same wrong claim appeared in the strategy step "Find the matching section in the passage. It
usually keeps the same order as the gaps", now "Find the part of the passage the task is drawn
from. The answers will usually all sit inside that one part, though not necessarily in the order
of the gaps." (fixed in both the lesson and reading-strategies.ts).

### 2. Invented rubric for the box variant

Before: "Choose the correct letter, A-J, from the box below for each answer." Real papers say
"Choose your answers from the box below and write the correct letter, A-J". Corrected, and one
genuinely useful fact added: the words in the box are usually paraphrases, not the passage's exact
wording. Students who hunt the box words verbatim in the text get nowhere.

### 3. Word-counting rules added for Variant 1.

## Things I did not change, and why

1. The band conversion table. It matches the conversion IELTS publishes, and the page already says
   it is approximate and can shift between versions. IELTS only publishes anchor points
   (15 = band 5, 23 = band 6, 30 = band 7, 35 = band 8); the table's rows agree with those
   anchors, so I left it.
2. The example passages themselves. Every one is relabelled "Example passage", but I did not edit
   the prose, because src/data/reading-practice.ts keys its interactive questions and explanations
   to these exact passages and another agent was rewriting that file at the same time. Editing the
   passages would have desynchronised the two. Two content problems in them need a follow-up pass
   done together with that file:
   - reading-diagram.html, About Ants. The passage says the thorax "is located before the head".
     The thorax is behind the head. reading-practice.ts quotes this exact sentence back at the
     student in an explanation, so both need fixing in one change.
   - reading-sentence.html, Pyramid Building. It credits the first smooth-sided pyramid to
     Imhotep, "an architect commissioned by King Sneferu". Imhotep built the Step Pyramid for
     Djoser, roughly a century before Sneferu. I could not establish whether this sentence came
     from the scraped source or was written here, so I left it rather than silently rewriting text
     that may be a quoted original, but it is wrong as history.
3. Whether "answers are in passage order" holds for Matching Features. Third-party guides say they
   are not in order; I could not find that in official wording. I phrased the lesson as practical
   advice ("Do not assume the answers run in passage order") rather than asserting a rule.
4. The passage boxes have no questions attached in any lesson. That is a teaching gap rather than
   an inaccuracy, and the interactive drills that supply the questions live in
   reading-practice.ts, outside this audit's scope. Worth a look once that file settles.
