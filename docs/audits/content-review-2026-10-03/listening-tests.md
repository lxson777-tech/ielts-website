## How it was checked

1. A read-only script dumped every paper and practice set and flagged: answers over the printed word limit
   (a number counts as one item, a hyphenated word as one word), letter keys not among the options, an
   explanation naming a different letter from the key, keys not found in the transcript (allowing digits vs
   words, plurals, British and American spelling), duplicate or missing ids, numbering out of 1 to 40,
   question-paper blanks that do not match the questions, Russian files with unknown ids or different letters,
   placeholders and broken markup, and practice keys that differ from their source paper.
2. A second script compared our key with the publisher's own answer panel (the cached publisher pages the
   importer keeps). Only the two September corrections differed.
3. Four helpers read every question of every paper against its transcript and reported suspect keys with the
   deciding line. I re-read every item before changing anything.
4. The useful rules are now a permanent test, `tests/listening-answer-key.test.ts` (8 checks): word limits
   counted the IELTS way, letter keys among the choices, a full key scores 40 and a blank scores 0, an
   explanation that says "matching answer X" or "Это вариант X" agrees with the key, lesson exercises never
   come from the reserved check papers, and every lesson exercise keeps its source paper's key.

## Band score conversion

`listeningBand()` in `src/lib/tests/schema.ts` uses: 39 = 9.0, 37 = 8.5, 35 = 8.0, 32 = 7.5, 30 = 7.0,
26 = 6.5, 23 = 6.0, 18 = 5.5, 16 = 5.0, 13 = 4.5, 11 = 4.0, 8 = 3.5, 6 = 3.0, 4 = 2.5.
ielts.org ("IELTS scoring in detail") publishes only the anchors Band 5 = 16, Band 6 = 23, Band 7 = 30,
Band 8 = 35, and says the exact marks vary slightly by test version. All four anchors match. The fuller table
that IDP and British Council material and most prep sites reproduce is identical from 9.0 down to 4.5, but
puts **Band 4.0 at 10 to 12**, where ours starts at 11. A student with 10/40 is shown 3.5 here and 4.0 in that
table. One-point difference, low impact; listed under "Seen but outside my files" because the table lives in
`schema.ts`.

## Seen but outside my files

1. **`src/lib/tests/schema.ts`, `normalizeAnswer`** (root cause of most "missing variant" reports). IELTS
   accepts numbers in figures or words, and phone numbers or codes with or without spaces or hyphens. The
   marker folds neither, so I added word forms to the data by rule. A one-place fix: fold whole-word number
   words (one to ninety-nine) to digits, drop spaces and hyphens between digits, and treat "10:45" and "10.45"
   alike. Then the generated data variants become harmless duplicates.
2. **`src/lib/tests/schema.ts`, `SPELLING_PAIRS`** lacks organiser/organizer, fertiliser/fertilizer,
   co-operate/cooperate, co-ordinator/coordinator, eardrum/ear drum style one-word forms. I added them per
   question; adding the pairs fixes it everywhere, Reading too.
3. **`src/lib/tests/schema.ts`, `LISTENING_BAND_TABLE`**: `[11, 4.0]` would be `[10, 4.0]` to match the widely
   published table (see "Band score conversion"). Low impact.
4. **`src/components/TestPlayer.tsx`, `countWords()`** counts a number as a word, so a correct "48 North
   Avenue" under "TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" shows a false "over the limit" warning. The word-limit reader in
   `tests/listening-answer-key.test.ts` (`fitsLimit`) is a tested model of the right rule.
5. **`tools/import_listening.py`, `ANSWER_OVERRIDES`**: today's key corrections are not in the importer's
   override table, so a re-import would bring the publisher's errors back. Re-importing would also drop all the
