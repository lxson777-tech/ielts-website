# IELTS is EZ Launch and Monetization Plan

Prepared for Alex on 30 September 2026. Access model confirmed on 1 October 2026.

**Agreed model:** all lesson explanations, strategies and worked examples are public and free, without an account. Practice and AI require an account with trial, paid or complimentary access. Launch IELTS is EZ for new customers in Kazakhstan, preserve complimentary practice for Alex’s existing students, and prove profitable customer acquisition before expanding advertising.

The first commercial target is **10 external paying customers who receive what they bought**, followed by evidence that some eligible customers choose to renew. A large audience, a beautiful homepage and many registrations are useful only if they help produce that result.

This plan uses a **200,000 KZT monthly marketing budget**, with 100,000 and 300,000 KZT alternatives. These are spending proposals, not authorizations. Kazakhstan first, the initial audience, workload and numerical performance targets below are recommendations. Alex approved the revised 12,990 KZT offer and assessment allowances on 30 September. Marketing allocations remain proposals.

The plan covers finishing the platform, hosting and payment, positioning, pricing, Instagram, Threads, partnerships, search, customer retention, measurement and a 90-day rollout. No advertising, outreach, purchases or production changes have been performed as part of preparing it.

## 2 Who should buy and why

**First audience to test:** adults in Kazakhstan preparing for Academic IELTS, roughly ages 18 to 30, aiming for 6.5 to 7.5, with an exam in the next 6 to 12 weeks. Focus first on people who already study independently or have lessons but need more practice and feedback between lessons. These are audience hypotheses, not verified market research findings.

Start with Russian marketing and a complete Russian buying journey, while keeping English practice and the existing English interface available. Add Kazakh campaigns after a fluent reviewer checks the entire journey. Do not divide a small budget between three languages at launch.

**Proposed positioning:** “A clear daily IELTS study plan, useful AI feedback, and realistic practice in one place.”

The demonstration must make this concrete: a learner completes an answer, sees one specific weakness, receives an explanation, practises the correction, and knows the next task. Sell that useful sequence. “Powered by AI” alone is easy for competitors to copy.

**Main customer problems and corresponding demonstrations:**

| Customer problem | What we show |
| --- | --- |
| “I do not know what to study today.” | A short daily session based on target, deadline and previous work. |
| “I write essays but do not know what to fix.” | One real example of feedback followed by a meaningful revision. |
| “I freeze when I have to speak.” | A real speaking interaction and the resulting practice advice. |
| “Free resources are scattered everywhere.” | Lessons, practice and progress connected in one student journey. |
| “I am not sure I can trust an AI score.” | Explain that scores are estimates, show the criteria and demonstrate teacher review of sample feedback. |

A second audience, introduced only after the adult journey works, is parents paying for teenage learners. Build parent-facing explanations and the appropriate consent process first. Meta restricts advertising to teens; do not plan interest-based targeting of minors. [Meta’s teen advertising rules](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/01/age-appropriate-ads-for-teens/)

Before substantial advertising, hold **15 discovery conversations with external potential buyers**. Recruit through permitted community posts, teacher introductions and opt-in responses to content. Ask what they use now, exam date, last preparation purchase, why they paid, where they get stuck and what would make this product worth 12,990 KZT. Then show the actual product and invite a real purchase when checkout is ready. Compliments and hypothetical willingness to pay are not sales.

## 6 How to put the paid platform online

**Recommended hosting direction:** assess Cloudflare Pages for the public website and the existing Cloudflare services for server functions, while resolving personal-data storage with the adviser. GitHub can remain the place where source code is stored.

GitHub Pages explicitly restricts hosting an online business or commercial software service. Moving the paid service is a launch requirement; attaching a domain to GitHub Pages is insufficient. [GitHub Pages limits](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)

The public website and the student database are separate hosting decisions. Choosing Cloudflare for the website does not establish that the student records meet Kazakhstan requirements.

**Release sequence:**

1. Confirm the exact domain spelling, business ownership and renewal cost. Check availability and relevant brand conflicts before buying. Domain suggestions are not availability claims.
2. Choose a single main address. Keep the website, prices and student platform under one coherent brand. Reserve a separate preview address for tests.
3. Prepare the reviewed release in the preview environment. Keep simulated checkout unavailable to real customers and prevent preview pages from search indexing.
4. Configure the domain and secure connection. Update internal links, canonical addresses, sitemap and the current GitHub Pages path prefix.
5. Configure Google sign-in redirects, password-reset links, outgoing mail, API allowed origins, payment-return addresses, provider notifications and any bot checks for the new domain.
6. Plan media delivery. Cloudflare Pages has a 25 MiB single-file limit; larger audio needs appropriate storage. Protected material must stay private and use the existing controlled-access approach. The current candidate has no individual public file above 25 MiB, so this is a deployment constraint rather than an identified size blocker. Private listening delivery is already designed in the paid candidate. Do not solve future size limits by exposing paid media publicly. [Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)
7. Back up student data and rehearse database changes. Ensure the previous working release and a recovery procedure are available. The current deployment configuration still defaults to open access unless paid mode is explicitly set. Set compatible access modes and content/payment addresses, apply access and support database changes, upload private material, and deploy the content, payment, tutor and grading services together. Verify that lessons and selected worked examples are publicly readable while protected practice questions, answer keys, full model banks and test recordings remain inaccessible without the correct account access. Recovery must preserve real orders and student work.
8. Move to the real payment provider’s test environment, then run the full student and payment checks. Include iPhone Safari, Android Chrome and Instagram’s in-app browser, with microphone permission denial, network interruption and return from checkout.
9. Show Alex the actual running preview with a concrete release checklist, costs and unresolved issues.
10. After Alex’s release approval, publish and perform a small, explicitly approved real payment and AI check. Verify access from another device and verify provider records.
11. Enable sales only when the checks pass. Keep advertising paused if checkout or the advertised AI service is unhealthy.
12. Preserve useful old links with supported redirects or clear forwarding pages, update the sitemap and verify the new domain in Google Search Console. GitHub Pages has limited redirect control; test what is actually possible instead of assuming server redirects exist.
13. Monitor the first week daily: availability, payment confirmations, failed activities, support and service cost.

Move useful local learning history into the account before changing domains where possible, because browser-only storage does not automatically follow a new domain. Rehearse both signed-in and signed-out cases and explain any limitation to current students.

The domain, provider approval, data review and real-device checks determine the date. Do not announce a fixed paid launch date before these dependencies are known.

Regardless of provider, the system must verify payment on the server, meaning in the trusted service behind the website. A successful-looking page in the student’s browser cannot grant paid access by itself.

Confirm with the payment provider and accountant what fiscal receipt is required and how it is issued. The existing printable order receipt is not proof that this obligation is satisfied. Each account should show plan, payment status, start date, expiry date and receipt reference. Buying another pass should extend access predictably. Refunded, duplicated and disputed transactions need an auditable record. Complimentary grants need their own record and reason, without fake revenue.

## 9 The route from an ad to a satisfied buyer

**Recommended journey:**

Relevant post, search result or ad → useful public lesson or focused landing page → practice invitation → account creation → explicitly started three-day trial → useful feedback or test result → purchase → continuing study → renewal or referral.

Every channel should lead to one relevant page, not force the visitor to search through the whole website. The first ads can use three versions of the same page: writing feedback, speaking practice and daily study plan. Reuse the existing approved design and keep the promise consistent with the creative.

**Landing page checklist:**

- One headline naming the learning problem.
- A short real product demonstration visible early on a phone.
- One primary action matched to the visitor’s intent: read the public lesson, or sign in to try practice. Explain trial limits and show the paid price before purchase.
- Exactly what access includes, its duration and any safeguards.
- What AI estimates can and cannot tell the student.
- Honest teacher credibility, operator details and a usable support contact.
- Answers about level, Academic IELTS, payment, expiry and devices.
- Real testimonials only when obtained with permission. At zero customers, use labelled examples instead.

Preserve the earlier preference not to put Alex’s personal identity at the centre of the brand. Use the previously approved factual training statement if still desired. Do not publish the private certificate or imply IDP endorsement.

**First-session objective:** within roughly 10 minutes, the learner understands one useful thing to improve and can act on it. This is a design target. Time it in observed sessions.

A full reading or listening test can remain available, but it should not be the only way to experience value. Start with a relevant short lesson or the existing trial writing or speaking experience, according to the learner’s chosen need. Do not create a new free assessment entitlement without updating the agreed trial rules.

**Trial follow-up, where the student has opted in to marketing:**

| Moment | Message purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Trial started | Explain the next small task and when practice and AI trial access ends. |
| First useful result | Point to one improvement action, not a generic sales message. |
| Next day | Encourage a second relevant activity; offer help if stuck. |
| Final day | Explain remaining time, the 12,990 KZT monthly offer and what continues after purchase. Lessons remain free. |
| After expiry | One useful invitation to continue practice, then stop repeated sales nudges if there is no response. |

Separate essential account and payment messages from optional marketing. Make it easy to unsubscribe. Keep saved results and expired-access behavior clear rather than using surprise data loss to encourage payment.

## 12 Threads and organic discovery

Threads should earn attention through useful conversations. It is not simply a place to copy Instagram captions.

**Starting rhythm:** five useful posts a week, plus 15 to 20 minutes of relevant replies on working days. This is a workload proposal. Reduce frequency if replies become generic or begin taking time from customer support.

Use four repeatable formats:

- One IELTS misconception, explained with a short example.
- A weak sentence or speaking answer and why a revision helps.
- A practical preparation question, such as which section is hardest to practise alone.
- An honest product demonstration or lesson learned from helping a learner.

Example posts:

“Learning a difficult synonym does not always improve an IELTS essay. The useful question is whether the word expresses your meaning accurately. Here is a simple example…”

“If you had 25 minutes for IELTS today, what would you practise first? Our first step is to look at the last mistake, then choose a task that addresses it.”

“An AI band estimate is a practice signal. The useful part is the explanation you can apply to your next answer. This is what that looks like in IELTS is EZ.”

Use a product link when it genuinely answers the topic, roughly once or twice a week initially. Add a distinct tracking label to the profile link. Reply to relevant public discussions respectfully; do not mass-tag, auto-reply, scrape users or send unsolicited promotional messages to students.

## 16 Unit economics and realistic revenue targets

**Acquisition cost** is the amount spent to obtain one new external paying customer. Calculate both media-only cost and the full cash cost including creator fees, referral rewards and creative expenses. Complimentary students and returning buyers do not belong in the new-customer denominator.

**Contribution** is what remains after serving the customer, before fixed overhead and profit. This is an operating model, not a tax calculation.

For the approved 12,990 KZT pass, the conservative planning illustration assumes all included assessments are used:

| --- | ---: |
| Price collected | 12,990 |
| Payment fee allowance at 3% | 390 |
| AI and variable delivery | 4,500 |
| Variable support labour provision | 700 |
| Refund, failure and recovery provision | 400 |
| External trial delivery allocated per new buyer | 1,500 |
| Contribution before acquisition and fixed costs | 5,500 |
| Target full acquisition cost | 3,000 |
| Remaining before fixed overhead and tax | 2,500 |

These are unmeasured planning assumptions, not provider quotes or guaranteed margins. The AI allowance assumes 12 essays at 100 KZT, 6 recordings at 200 KZT, 2 live interviews with feedback at 900 KZT, and 300 KZT of tutor use: 4,500 KZT total. The trial allocation assumes 150 KZT per external trial and 10% trial-to-paid conversion, so 1,500 KZT per buyer. Track all unsuccessful trials, failures and retries. A 550 KZT/USD budgeting rate was used in the earlier cost analysis; it is not a current exchange-rate quote. Replace these assumptions with provider billing and observed usage before scaling. Complimentary students are a separate operating cost.

At 3,000 KZT full acquisition cost, approximately 2,500 KZT remains per first purchase before overhead and tax. At 5,000 KZT acquisition cost only about 500 KZT remains. At 5,500 KZT essentially nothing remains for overhead. A 1,500 KZT delivery-cost increase reduces the target surplus to about 1,000 KZT. At a hypothetical 9,990 KZT price, the same assumptions produce a loss of about 410 KZT before overhead and tax.

These are management targets, not market benchmarks. Do not release the full monthly marketing budget until a small paid cohort demonstrates positive contribution. Reconcile provider usage and invoices, payment charges, customer support time and trial costs weekly.

Do not sell the former 25,000 KZT 90-day package at launch. Three months of full-use delivery can erase the discount's margin. Reconsider a 90-day price only after measuring monthly usage, exam-related churn and actual acquisition cost. The previously discussed 34,990 KZT is a possible later test, not an active or approved quarterly offer.

## 17 Retention and support

IELTS preparation naturally ends when the exam is over. Do not assume that students remain subscribed for a year. Since access does not renew automatically, evaluate whether eligible learners deliberately buy again.

**Daily experience:** one clear next task, saved progress, specific feedback and a useful next action. Avoid excessive reminders or streaks that add pressure without improving practice.

**Weekly experience:** show what the learner actually completed, the weakness they worked on and the next recommended focus. Separate completed work from estimated scores. Never invent improvement from a target band.

**Support at launch:** provide a visible WhatsApp Business or email route, state actual staffed hours and aim to answer within one working day. Do not promise 24/7 support without staffing it. Record the reason for each contact: payment, login, audio, feedback, confusion or something else.

Use an internal incident procedure: identify the affected service, pause related sales or ads if necessary, inform affected users appropriately, restore service and record the root cause. Define how failed paid activities or outages are handled in the access policy.

Before access ends, send a factual expiry reminder through the appropriate permitted channel. Explain the price to continue and preserve the no-auto-renewal promise. After expiry, provide a clear way to return and explain what happens to stored work.

**Retention measures:** return for another meaningful activity within seven days; number of useful study sessions; support burden; completion of practice after feedback; and second purchase among customers whose first pass has ended. Keep customers who finished their exam separate from customers who left because the product disappointed them. The 90-day package is paused. Measure repeat purchases only among customers whose 30-day access has actually ended.

Ask five non-renewers why they stopped. Fix the most common preventable reason before adding another acquisition channel.

## 18 Measurement and weekly decisions

Keep four groups separate: existing complimentary students, external trial students, external paying customers and returning paying customers.

Use the platform’s trusted payment records as the source of sales truth. Add a simple weekly spreadsheet summary. Choose one web analytics setup after the privacy review; GA4 plus appropriate Meta conversion measurement is a reasonable candidate, not a mandate to send all student behavior to advertising companies.

**Minimum event plan:**

| Event | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Landing visit | A visitor reaches a campaign destination. |
| Public lesson read | A visitor meaningfully engages with a free lesson; this is not a trial start or a sale. |
| Practice invitation clicked | A lesson reader chooses to try practice or AI. |
| Signup completed | An account is actually created. |
| Profile completed | The required personal profile is finished. Measure abandonment before the trial separately. |
| Trial started | The student explicitly starts the timed trial. |
| First useful activity completed | The benefit promised by the campaign is delivered. For a feedback ad, feedback arrives and the student reaches a clear next action. Track general lesson engagement separately. |
| Checkout started | The customer begins paying for a specified pass. |
| Payment confirmed | The trusted provider record confirms settled or valid successful payment according to the integration. |
| Access granted | The customer can actually use the purchased plan. |
| Second meaningful session | The student returns and uses the product. |
| Access extended | An existing buyer buys again. |
| Refund or reversal | Revenue and access are adjusted correctly. |

Deduplicate payments with a unique order reference. Do not count a checkout-button click or thank-you page view as revenue. Where browser and server tracking both report an event, prevent double counting.

Campaign labels should identify source, campaign and creative, for example Instagram, launch-writing and feedback-demo-01. Store only what is needed. Never put names, phone numbers, essay text, recordings or private feedback into campaign URLs or advertising analytics.

**Proposed early review targets:**

- At least 50% of trial starters complete a useful first activity.
- At least 15% of trial starters buy within seven days of trial start.
- At least 40% of new paid customers return for another meaningful session within seven days.
- Full cash acquisition cost trends toward 3,000 KZT or less.
- Payment success and access delivery agree for every observed purchase.
- No unresolved severe privacy, payment or data-loss issue.

These are deliberately explicit test thresholds, not sourced market averages or guarantees. Review raw counts beside percentages. Five trials are too few to treat a percentage as a stable result.

**Weekly review, 30 minutes:**

1. Reconcile cash received, refunds and actual access.
2. Look at visits, trials, activation, purchases and return use by source.
3. Review AI cost, failed calls, free-student usage and support time.
4. Read the week’s buyer objections and support problems.
5. Choose one principal improvement and one marketing test.
6. Release, hold or reduce spending.

If many visitors do not start, investigate message, page and trust. If many start but do not experience value, fix onboarding or the product. If active trial users do not buy, examine value, price and payment friction. If buyers leave, fix the experience before buying more traffic.

## 19 The 90 day rollout

Dates are relative to starting implementation. The public launch moves if payment onboarding, legal review or reliability takes longer. Marketing spend begins when the corresponding launch conditions are met.

| Period | Work and deliverables | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 7 | Confirm operator/support details, request payment proposals, commission specific data/refund review, identify release candidate, define complimentary access, create brand assets and research 20 partners. Begin discovery conversations. | Named owners and dependencies; one chosen payment direction; one audience and offer. |
| Days 8 to 14 | Integrate payment test mode, implement complimentary grants and tracking, prepare new-domain preview, check accounts/audio/expiry/support, prepare three ad concepts and six to nine useful posts. | All critical pilot checks pass on the preview; merchant and legal blockers resolved or explicitly outstanding. |
| Days 15 to 21 | After release approval, publish on suitable hosting, run approved real payment and AI checks, invite the first external buyers through permitted partner and organic activity. | Aim for 5 to 10 external paid users; every actual purchase delivers access; problems are recorded and fixed. |
| Days 22 to 30 | Release the first capped Instagram test, continue Threads and partner conversations, observe first sessions, review the complete conversion journey. | A first sales and cost report, including failures and actual spending. Do not force the buyer target by counting free students. |
| Days 31 to 45 | Improve the biggest measured obstacle, produce stronger demonstrations from real product use, run the next controlled creative or page test. | Repeatable external purchases and a credible route toward acceptable acquisition cost. |
| Days 46 to 60 | Review first 30-day renewals, service costs and support. Test one additional channel, such as Google Search or a creator placement, only if the main journey works. | Renewal evidence and a channel comparison using sales, cost and effort. |
| Days 61 to 90 | Concentrate spending on the strongest offer/channel combination; consider one teacher partnership pilot; improve retention and onboarding from evidence. | Decide to grow, reposition or pause paid acquisition. |

**Commercial milestones:** 10 external buyers first; then 30 cumulative external buyers; then 50 active paying customers with acceptable delivery cost. Reaching all three in 90 days is an objective, not a forecast. Customer lifetime and renewal determine the difference between cumulative sales and active customers.

**Decision at day 30:** do external learners understand the offer, receive value and pay?

**Decision at day 60:** are purchases repeatable, are eligible students renewing, and can acquisition be paid back from observed contribution?

**Decision at day 90:** is there a credible profitable path? If yes, grow within capacity. If useful trials consistently fail to become sales after two clear offer tests, revise audience, value or pricing before funding more traffic. If buyers like the platform but advertising is too expensive, emphasize partnerships and search. If service costs break the economics, revise delivery or the offer before increasing sales.
