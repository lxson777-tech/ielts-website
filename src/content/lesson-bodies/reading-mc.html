Multiple Choice Questions

What is it?

The official name for this task is Multiple Choice. You are given a question, or the first half of a sentence, with four answer options (A, B, C, D). You must locate the information in the passage and choose the correct option. Some versions give a longer list of options and ask for more than one answer, for example two answers from five options (A-E) or three from seven (A-G); the instructions always say how many to choose, and each correct answer is worth one mark. Always write a letter as your answer. Never words.

Two Forms

  • Form A: A question with four possible answers. E.g. "What is the main reason…?"
  • Form B: The first half of a sentence with four possible endings. E.g. "Rice is difficult to grow because…"

Both require the same skill: locate and understand meaning. Form B is not the same task as Matching Sentence Endings, where one lettered list of endings is shared across several sentence beginnings.

How to Approach It

  1. Read the question and all of the options carefully before looking at the passage.
  2. Identify differences between the options. They may be subtly different.
  3. Prepare paraphrases and synonyms for the question keywords.
  4. Scan the passage for the relevant section (answers come in order).
  5. Read the surrounding sentences. Not just the one that matches.
  6. You may see information about several of the options in the passage. Unless the instructions ask for more than one answer, only one is correct.
  7. Choose based on deeper meaning, not just word-matching.
⚠ Trap: every option may appear somewhere in the passage. Appearing is not the same as answering the question ⚠ Trap: similar-sounding options with small but crucial differences
A real one, to show the format. From Academic Reading Test 7, Question 38. The instruction reads "Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D." and the question is "What does the writer suggest about ABS in the fifth paragraph?" The four options are: A It is bound to make key decisions that are wrong. B It may reduce some of the appeal of the game. C It will lead to the disappearance of human umpires. D It may increase calls for the rules of baseball to be changed. The answer is B, and what you write on the answer sheet is the single letter B.
