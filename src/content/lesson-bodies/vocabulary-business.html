Topic Vocabulary

Business & Entrepreneurship

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
entrepreneura person who starts a business and accepts the financial risk"Many entrepreneurs fail several times before they succeed."
start-upa new company, usually small and recently founded"The start-up grew from three employees to two hundred in a year."
profit marginthe difference between what something costs to make and what it sells for"Supermarkets operate on very narrow profit margins."
multinational corporationa large company that operates in several countries"Multinational corporations can move their profits to low-tax countries."
small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs)small and medium businesses, which employ most workers in most economies"Small and medium-sized enterprises create the majority of new jobs."
competitionrivalry between businesses selling similar products"Competition between airlines has driven ticket prices down."
customer servicethe help and treatment a company gives the people who buy from it"Poor customer service quickly destroys a company's reputation."
supply and demandthe relationship between how much is available and how much people want"Prices are determined largely by supply and demand."
business modelthe way a company plans to make money"Their business model depends on selling subscriptions rather than products."
market sharethe proportion of total sales in a market that one company has"The firm has lost market share to cheaper rivals."

red tapeofficial rules and paperwork that make things slow and difficult"Excessive red tape discourages people from starting a business."
bankruptcythe legal state of being unable to pay what you owe"The chain went into bankruptcy after three poor years."

Key Collocations

  • set up / run a business
  • make a profit / make a loss
  • break into a market
  • go bankrupt
  • cut costs
  • gain a competitive edge

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "Supporting small businesses is often the most effective way to create local jobs."
  • "Large corporations have a responsibility that extends beyond their shareholders."
  • "Entrepreneurship should be encouraged at school, since not every graduate will find an employer."
  • "Where one company dominates a market, consumers almost always pay more."
  • "Governments can encourage enterprise by reducing the paperwork facing new firms."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. Investors provide ________ to new companies in the hope of a large future return. (venture capital)
  2. Where a single firm holds a ________, customers have nowhere else to go. (monopoly)
  3. ________ such as local shops and workshops employ most of the population. (Small and medium-sized enterprises)
  4. Excessive ________ discourages people from turning a good idea into a business. (red tape)
  5. A company's ________ explains exactly how it intends to make money. (business model)
  6. Under ________, firms are expected to consider their effect on society as well as their profits. (corporate social responsibility)
  7. The airline lost ________ to competitors offering the same routes for less. (market share)
  8. Moving production abroad, or ________, reduced costs but cost jobs at home. (outsourcing)
  9. Anyone who starts a company has to ________ that it may not succeed. (take the risk)
  10. After three years of losses, the chain was forced into ________. (bankruptcy)
