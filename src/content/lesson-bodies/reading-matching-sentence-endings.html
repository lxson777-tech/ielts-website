Matching Sentence Endings

What is it?

The official name for this task is Matching Sentence Endings. On the paper it reads something like: "Complete each sentence with the correct ending, A-H, below." You are given a numbered list of sentence beginnings and a lettered list of possible endings. You must join each beginning to the ending that correctly completes it, according to the passage.

Key Rules

  • Your answer is always a letter
  • There are always more endings than beginnings, so some endings are never used
  • The correct ending must be grammatically correct and factually correct. An ending can read perfectly well and still be wrong
  • Answers follow the order of the passage
  • The rubric gives the letter range, for example A-H. Every answer must be one of those letters

How to Approach It

  1. Read every beginning first, and check what grammatical form each one needs to continue naturally (a verb? a noun phrase? a reason?).
  2. Read every ending and note its grammatical form too, before you match anything.
  3. Eliminate any ending whose grammar simply cannot follow a given beginning, even if the topic looks related.
  4. Scan the passage for the section covering each beginning's topic. Answers appear in passage order.
  5. Confirm the surviving ending against the passage's actual facts. Do not choose an ending purely because the sentence sounds fluent.
⚠ Trap: an ending that fits grammatically but states a fact the passage never gives, or contradicts it ⚠ Trap: two endings that both sound plausible for the same beginning. Only the passage decides ⚠ Trap: reusing an ending because it "sounds right" for more than one beginning
Not the same as Sentence Completion. Here you choose an ending from a lettered list, so your answer is always a letter and the wording is already written for you. In Sentence Completion you write the missing words yourself, taken directly from the passage and kept within a set word limit. Do not paraphrase an ending in your own words here, and do not copy a lettered ending into a Sentence Completion gap.
Worked example: the beginning "Fleming noticed that a mould growing in one of his dishes had…" needs an ending in the past tense describing what the mould did. An ending such as "…refused to believe the mould had any medicinal value" cannot follow grammatically, because a mould cannot refuse to believe anything, so it is eliminated before you even check the facts. Only once the grammar rules an ending in or out do you confirm the survivor against what the passage actually says.
