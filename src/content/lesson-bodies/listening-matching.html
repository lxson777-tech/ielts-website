Matching

What is it?

  • The official name is Matching
  • The instruction line reads something like: "What does the speaker say about each of the following classes? Choose FIVE answers from the box and write the correct letter, A-G, next to questions 16-20."
  • You are given a list of items (people, places, courses, topics) and a box of statements, and you match each item to the statement that describes it
  • Answer format: a single letter for each numbered question
  • Appears most often in Parts 2 and 3

Key Rules

  • There are usually more statements than questions, so one or two are never used. That is deliberate, not a mistake
  • Each statement, once used, is normally used only once, unless the instructions say a letter may be used more than once, which does occasionally happen
  • Answers follow the order the items are mentioned in the recording, not the order they are listed in the box
  • The wording in the box is almost always a paraphrase of what you hear, never a word-for-word match

How to Approach It

  1. In the pause before this section, read the list of items first, then read every statement in the box and think of a possible paraphrase for each one.
  2. Underline the one or two key words in each statement that make it different from the others; those are the words whose synonyms you are listening for.
  3. Track the items in the order given, since the recording moves through them one after another and rarely jumps back.
  4. When you hear an idea that matches a statement's meaning, note the letter lightly and keep listening. Do not assume the exact words will appear.
  5. At the end, at least one statement in the box should be left over. If you have used every single one, go back and check the two answers you are least sure of.
⚠ Trap: the box contains one or two statements that are never used at all ⚠ Trap: two statements describe similar-sounding situations, and only careful listening tells them apart ⚠ Trap: the recording uses a synonym for every key word in the box, never the box's own wording
Transcript. Read at a steady pace, as if listening. (Simulation: This Term's Evening Classes)

Course Advisor: Right, let me run quickly through this term's evening classes before you sign up. Pottery is completely full already, I'm afraid, so if you're keen you'll need to go on the waiting list for January, when a second group starts. Photography still has plenty of spaces, and it's genuinely ideal for beginners, since the first four weeks cover nothing but camera basics before anyone touches editing software. French continues directly from last term's syllabus, so unfortunately it's only open to students who attended in the autumn, we can't take newcomers halfway through. Yoga has actually moved to the larger hall this term because of demand, and it now runs twice a week instead of just once, so there's more flexibility if Tuesdays don't suit you. Finally, cookery is the one class where you'll need to bring your own ingredients each week according to a list we email out, every other class supplies all its own materials and equipment. Right, any questions before we move on to fees?
