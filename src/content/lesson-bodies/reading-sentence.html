Sentence Completion

What is it?

The official name for this task is Sentence Completion. You are given incomplete sentences with a gap (___). You must find the correct word(s) from the passage to complete each sentence. You must use exact words from the passage and stay within the word limit printed with the task. The limit changes from task to task, for example "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" or "ONE WORD ONLY", so read it every time.

Key Rules

  • Words must come directly from the passage
  • Obey the word limit strictly. 4 words when the limit is 3 = wrong
  • A hyphenated word such as "check-in" counts as one word, and a number counts as one word
  • Contracted words such as "they're" are never tested, so you will never need one
  • Spelling must be correct
  • Answers follow the order of the passage

How to Approach It

  1. Read each incomplete sentence and identify keywords before searching.
  2. Think about what type of word is missing (noun, verb, adjective, number?).
  3. Scan the passage using keywords and synonyms to locate the relevant section.
  4. Read carefully around that section and identify the exact word(s) that complete the sentence logically and grammatically.
  5. Write the answer. Check spelling and word count.
⚠ Trap: paraphrasing instead of copying exact words ⚠ Trap: going over the word limit ⚠ Trap: ignoring grammar. The completed sentence must make grammatical sense

Writing your answer

More marks are lost on the form of an answer than on understanding the passage. You find the right line, you understand it perfectly, and then you write it down in a way that cannot be accepted. Two habits fix nearly all of it: predict the word type before you search, then follow the answer rules when you copy.

Half 1. Predict the word type before you look

  • "a" or "the" in front of the gap means the answer is a noun. "A" tells you even more: the noun must be countable and singular, so an uncountable word such as "machinery", "equipment", "advice" or "research" is impossible there.
  • A preposition next to the gap tells you the shape of the answer. "...was fitted with a ___" needs a thing. "...depends on ___" needs a thing or an -ing form. When the gap comes first and a preposition follows it ("The ___ of the tower was rebuilt"), you need a noun again.
  • Read the finished sentence back to yourself with your answer inside it. If it does not sound like English, it is wrong, however well it matches the meaning.

Worked example: grammar alone decides it

Passage: "Early engineers solved the problem by fitting each tower with a revolving lens. The rotating machinery beneath it had to be wound by hand every two hours."

Question 7 (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS): "To make the beam sweep across the sea, engineers fitted each tower with a ___."

✗ Looks right, marked wrong
rotating machinery

By meaning it is tempting, because that is the part which makes the beam move. But "machinery" is uncountable, and "a machinery" is not English. The single word "a" rules it out before you even weigh up the meaning.

✓ Correct
revolving lens

A countable singular noun, so it fits after "a", it is the passage's own wording, and it is two words.

Half 2. The answer rules

Rule Marked wrong Marked right
Copy the words exactly as the passage prints them. Never tidy them into wording of your own.
Passage: "the moth's remarkable endurance"		 amazing stamina remarkable endurance
Keep singular or plural as printed. Do not change a word's number to make your sentence read more smoothly.
Passage: "the scheme paid farmers to restore hedgerows"		 hedgerow hedgerows
Write only the missing words. If the sentence already prints "the" or "and", do not write it again.
Sentence: "The device measures the ___ of the water."		 the salinity salinity
Keep a word that carries meaning, even when it makes the answer longer.
Passage: "the quieter students suffered from low self-confidence"		 self-confidence low self-confidence
Drop a pronoun or a comparative the sentence already contains.
Sentence: "Solar panels are now ___ than they were in 2010."
Passage: "far cheaper than they were a decade ago"		 cheaper than they were far cheaper
Include a preposition when the phrase needs one.
Sentence: "Maps help travellers ___ unfamiliar streets."
Passage: "maps help travellers make sense of unfamiliar streets"		 make sense make sense of
Count the words against the limit. A hyphenated word counts as one word, and a number written in digits counts as one.
Limit: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER		 a set of 20 purpose-built tanks 20 purpose-built tanks
Write one answer only. In the real test you never write two options, never use a slash, never use brackets. lens / mirror lens
