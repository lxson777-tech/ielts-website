Short-answer Questions

What is it?

The official name for this task is Short-answer Questions. On the paper it reads something like: "Answer the questions below. Choose NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER from the passage for each answer." You are asked direct questions about specific facts and must write the answer yourself, using words taken from the passage. It is set on factual, information-dense text, and it can appear with any of the three passages.

Key Rules

  • Never exceed the stated word limit. Read it every time, it changes between tests
  • A hyphenated word such as "space-based" counts as one word
  • Numbers can be written as figures (e.g. "1608") rather than spelled out, and a number counts as one word
  • Contracted words such as "they're" are never tested
  • Spelling must be correct. A correct word spelled wrongly is marked wrong
  • Answers follow the order of the passage
  • If a question asks "how many" or "how much", expect a number in the answer, not a written-out description

How to Approach It

  1. Read the question and underline exactly what it is asking for: a date, a name, a number, a reason, an object.
  2. Identify what part of speech or fact-type the answer must be. This narrows your search fast.
  3. Scan the passage in order for the relevant section. Short-answer questions almost always follow the passage's own sequence.
  4. Lift the exact word(s) that answer the question. Do not paraphrase them into your own words.
  5. Check the word count and the spelling before moving on.
  6. If your answer feels too long, reread the question. There is almost always a shorter, more precise word sitting in the passage.
⚠ Trap: writing more words than the limit allows by adding an unnecessary article or adjective ⚠ Trap: spelling a name or technical term from the passage incorrectly ⚠ Trap: answering with a paraphrase instead of the exact word the passage uses
Worked example: if a question asks "By how many times could Galileo's telescope magnify objects?", the answer counts as one word whether you write "twenty" or "20", so pick one and write only that. If a question asks what kind of instruments modern observatories rely on, the answer "space-based" counts as one word too, even though it contains a hyphen, so it still fits inside a one-word limit.

Writing your answer

More marks are lost on the form of an answer than on understanding the passage. You find the right line, you understand it perfectly, and then you write it down in a way that cannot be accepted. Two habits fix nearly all of it: predict the word type before you search, then follow the answer rules when you copy.

Half 1. Predict the word type before you look

  • The question carries the same clues a gap would. If it prints "a" or "the" in front of the missing item ("What is the name of the ___ that..."), the answer is a noun, and "a" means it must be a countable singular noun, so an uncountable word such as "equipment" or "machinery" is impossible there.
  • The question word itself fixes the shape. "How many" needs a number. "Where" needs a place. "What are visitors asked to do" needs a verb, not a thing. A preposition in the question ("What is the roof covered with?") narrows it further.
  • Turn the question into a statement, put your answer inside it, and read it back. If it does not sound like English, it is wrong, however well it matches the meaning.

Worked example: grammar alone decides it

Passage: "Before entering, visitors must clean their boots at the gate. A small entrance fee is payable at the kiosk."

Question 11 (NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS): "What are visitors asked to do before entering the cave?"

✗ Looks right, marked wrong
a small entrance fee

By meaning it is something visitors deal with before going in, and it sits in the same two lines of the passage. But "asked to do" has to be followed by a verb, and "visitors are asked to do a small entrance fee" is not English.

✓ Correct
clean their boots

Read it back: "Visitors are asked to clean their boots." It starts with a verb, so it fits the question, and it is three words.
