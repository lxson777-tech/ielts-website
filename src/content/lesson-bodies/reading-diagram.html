Diagram Label Completion

What is it?

The official name for this task is Diagram Label Completion. You are given a diagram (a drawing of a device, a structure, a process, or anything else the passage describes that can be shown as a picture) with numbered blank labels. You must find the correct word(s) from the passage to fill each blank. The answer is always a word or words taken directly from the passage.

Key Rules

  • You must use words from the passage: do not paraphrase
  • The word limit is printed with the task, for example "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER". Read it each time
  • A hyphenated word counts as one word, and a number counts as one word
  • Spelling must be correct
  • Unlike most types, the answers may not come in the same order as the information in the passage
  • The diagram shows a visual. Your job is to connect parts of the image to terminology in the text

How to Approach It

  1. Study the diagram first. What is it showing? What parts are labelled and what are blank?
  2. Read the passage and identify the section that describes this diagram.
  3. Match each blank to the position on the diagram. Think about location/function.
  4. Find the exact word(s) in the passage that name that part.
  5. Check the word limit. Never exceed it.
⚠ Trap: writing paraphrases instead of exact passage words ⚠ Trap: exceeding the word limit ⚠ Trap: misspelling technical terms

Writing your answer

More marks are lost on the form of an answer than on understanding the passage. You spot the right part of the diagram, you find the right line in the text, and then you write the label in a way that cannot be accepted. Two habits fix nearly all of it: predict the word type before you search, then follow the answer rules when you copy.

Half 1. Predict the word type before you look

  • Read the words the diagram already prints around the blank. An "a" or a "the" in front of the blank means the answer is a noun, and "a" means it must be a countable singular noun, so an uncountable word such as "insulation", "machinery" or "equipment" is impossible there.
  • A preposition in the label tells you the shape of the answer. "Air escapes through the ___" needs a thing. "Water flows into the ___" needs a thing. Where the blank comes before a printed word ("___ tank"), the answer is usually the describing word, not the thing itself.
  • Read the whole label back to yourself with your answer inside it. Diagram labels are short, so a wrong word type stands out at once. If it does not sound like English, it is wrong, however well it matches the picture.

Worked example: grammar alone decides it

Passage: "The heat is trapped by a glass cover, while foam insulation beneath the panel stops it escaping downwards."

Label 3 (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS): "Heat is trapped by a ___."

✗ Looks right, marked wrong
foam insulation

By meaning it is tempting, because keeping the heat in is exactly what insulation does. But "insulation" is uncountable, and "a foam insulation" is not English. The "a" printed on the diagram rules it out on its own.

✓ Correct
glass cover

A countable singular noun, so it fits after "a", it is the passage's own wording, and it is two words.

Half 2. The answer rules

Rule Marked wrong Marked right
Copy the words exactly as the passage prints them. The picture may suggest an obvious everyday name for a part, but only the passage's own words count.
Passage: "the outer casing is made of moulded plastic"		 plastic shell moulded plastic
Keep singular or plural as printed. Do not change a word's number because the diagram appears to show only one of something.
Passage: "air is drawn in through vents on both sides"		 vent vents
Write only the missing words. Diagram labels often print half the phrase already, and repeating it costs you the mark.
Label: "___ tank"
Passage: "a storage tank sits above the pump"		 storage tank storage
Keep a word that carries meaning, even when it makes the label longer.
Passage: "a fine mesh filter keeps insects out"		 filter mesh filter
Drop a pronoun or a comparative the label already contains.
Label: "The lower chamber is ___ than the upper one."
Passage: "considerably wider than the upper one"		 wider than the upper one considerably wider
Include a preposition when the phrase needs one.
Label: "Sensors ___ the changing light."
Passage: "sensors respond to the changing light"		 respond respond to
Count the words against the limit. A hyphenated word counts as one word, and a number written in digits counts as one.
Limit: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER		 a set of two rubber-lined rollers 2 rubber-lined rollers
Write one answer only. In the real test you never write two options, never use a slash, never use brackets. vents (or openings) vents
