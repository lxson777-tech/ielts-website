Topic Vocabulary

Media & Advertising

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
mass medianewspapers, television, radio and websites that reach very large audiences"The mass media has enormous power to shape public opinion."
press freedomthe right of journalists to report without government control"Press freedom is essential if those in power are to be held to account."
biased reportingnews coverage that unfairly favours one side"Biased reporting makes it hard for voters to form a balanced view."
tabloida popular newspaper that focuses on celebrities, scandal and short stories"The tabloids devoted ten pages to the footballer's private life."
broadsheeta serious newspaper that covers politics, business and world affairs in depth"Broadsheets have lost many readers to free online news."
censorshipthe official removal or banning of information, books or films"Internet censorship prevents citizens from reading foreign news."
target audiencethe particular group of people that a message or product is aimed at"The target audience for the campaign is teenagers."
advertising campaigna planned series of advertisements for one product or idea"The company spent millions on a global advertising campaign."
brand awarenesshow well the public knows and recognises a brand"Sponsoring a football team is a quick way to build brand awareness."
consumer behaviourthe way people choose, buy and use products"Advertising aims to influence consumer behaviour, often without people noticing."

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
sensationalismpresenting news in a shocking or exaggerated way to attract attention"Sensationalism sells newspapers but distorts the facts."
investigative journalismin-depth reporting that uncovers hidden wrongdoing"Investigative journalism exposed corruption at the highest level."
fake newsfalse stories that are presented as genuine news"Fake news travels faster online than any correction."
media literacythe ability to judge whether a source of information can be trusted"Schools should teach media literacy alongside reading and writing."
product placementpaying for a product to appear in a film or programme"Product placement is a subtle form of advertising."
targeted advertisingadvertisements chosen for an individual based on their personal data"Targeted advertising relies on tracking what users do online."
celebrity endorsementa famous person recommending a product in return for payment"A celebrity endorsement can double sales overnight."
misleading claimsstatements in an advertisement that give a false impression"The company was fined for making misleading claims about health benefits."
invasion of privacyan unjustified intrusion into a person's private life"Photographing a celebrity's children is widely seen as an invasion of privacy."
public broadcastera television or radio organisation funded by the public rather than by advertising"A public broadcaster can make programmes that would never attract advertisers."

Key Collocations

  • shape / sway public opinion
  • hold the powerful to account
  • launch an advertising campaign
  • be bombarded with advertisements
  • regulate advertising aimed at children
  • verify a source

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "The media plays a decisive role in shaping how the public views an issue."
  • "Advertising aimed at children is widely regarded as unethical because they cannot recognise persuasion."
  • "A free press is a cornerstone of any democratic society."
  • "Critics argue that advertising creates artificial needs rather than meeting real ones."
  • "Unless people learn to question their sources, false stories will continue to spread."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. Without ________, journalists cannot investigate those in power. (press freedom)
  2. A ________ by a famous athlete can make a pair of trainers sell out in days. (celebrity endorsement)
  3. ________ often attracts more readers than careful, balanced reporting. (Sensationalism)
  4. Teaching ________ helps young people check whether an online story is reliable. (media literacy)
  5. The firm was fined for ________ that its cereal could prevent heart disease. (misleading claims)
  6. Governments that practise ________ decide what their citizens are allowed to read. (censorship)
  7. ________ uses personal data to show each user the products they are most likely to buy. (Targeted advertising)
  8. Sponsoring a major sporting event is an expensive but effective way to build ________. (brand awareness)
  9. ________ that uncovers corruption is costly, which is why few newspapers still fund it. (Investigative journalism)
  10. The ________ for toy advertisements is young children, who cannot judge the claims being made. (target audience)
