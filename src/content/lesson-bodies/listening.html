About the Test

IELTS Listening at a Glance

The Basics

  • Total time: approximately 30 minutes, plus 10 minutes transfer time on the paper test, or a 2-minute check on the computer-delivered test
  • 40 questions across 4 parts, 10 questions in each part, 1 mark each
  • Each recording is played once only: no second chance
  • Recordings increase in difficulty from Part 1 to Part 4
  • A range of accents is used: British, Australian, New Zealand and North American
  • The same Listening test is taken by Academic and General Training candidates
  • No negative marking. Never leave a blank

Important: Spelling Counts A misspelled answer in Listening is marked wrong. Double-check your spelling before you finish: on the paper test use the 10 minutes of transfer time, on the computer-delivered test use the 2-minute check at the end. Especially for proper nouns, numbers, and specialist vocabulary.
Strategy

How to Approach the Test

Before Listening. Read Ahead

  • Use every second of reading time to preview the questions
  • Underline keywords in each question
  • Predict what type of information is needed: a name? a number? a place?
  • For form completion: predict the format (e.g. this blank needs a date)
  • For multiple choice: read all options and identify how they differ

While Listening

  • Follow the questions in order. Answers come in sequence
  • If you miss one, move on immediately. Don't fall behind
  • Write as you listen. Don't wait until the recording ends
  • Listen for signpost language: "Now I'd like to…", "Moving on to…"
  • Listen for corrections: "Actually…", "Sorry, I meant…". The corrected version is the answer
Learn Each Part

The Four Parts & Every Question Type

Each recording has its own lesson: what to expect, the question types it uses, key language, and a practice simulation with answers. There is also a lesson for every official question type, so you can drill one format across all four parts. Work through them in any order.
