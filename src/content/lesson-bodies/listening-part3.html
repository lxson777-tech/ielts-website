Part 3 · Medium-Hard

Academic Discussion

Most common question types here: multiple choice, matching and form/note/table/flow-chart completion.

What is it?

  • Conversation between up to four people in an educational or training context
  • Examples: students discussing an assignment, a student and a tutor reviewing a project
  • Typical question types: multiple choice, matching, sentence completion and note or table completion
  • The challenge: tracking who says what, through heavy paraphrasing

The Distractor Game

  • The speakers will usually mention every option: only one answers the question asked
  • Listen for correction language: "actually…", "I mean…", "no, wait…". The corrected version is the answer
  • Opinions shift during the conversation: the answer is where they end up, not where they start
  • Study-related vocabulary pays off here. Assignments, sources, deadlines, tutorials, drafts

Strategy

  1. In the preview, read each question and underline what exactly is asked: cause? recommendation? problem?
  2. Note how the options differ from each other; that difference is what you listen for.
  3. Match the meaning, not the words. The recording almost never uses the option's wording.
  4. Keep moving: a missed question costs one mark, falling behind costs several.
