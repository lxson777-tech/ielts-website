Topic Vocabulary

Food & Diet

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
balanced dieteating the right amounts of different kinds of food to stay healthy"A balanced diet includes plenty of fruit, vegetables and protein."
processed foodfood that has been changed in a factory, often with added salt, sugar or fat"Processed food is cheap and convenient but usually high in salt."
fast foodhot food, such as burgers and chips, that is prepared and served quickly"Fast food outlets now outnumber grocery shops in some areas."
home-cooked mealfood prepared at home from fresh ingredients"Busy families rarely have time for a home-cooked meal."
organic producefruit, vegetables and other food grown without artificial chemicals"Organic produce is healthier for the soil but costs more."
food wastefood that is thrown away instead of being eaten"Supermarkets are under pressure to cut food waste."
eating habitswhat, when and how a person usually eats"Eating habits formed in childhood tend to last a lifetime."
cuisinethe style of cooking of a particular country or region"Italian cuisine is popular all over the world."
staple fooda basic food that people in a region eat almost every day"Rice is the staple food for more than half of the world's population."
convenience foodready-made meals that need little or no preparation"Sales of convenience food rise as working hours get longer."

Ten more words for the same topic. Learn these once the table above feels comfortable.

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
food securityreliable access to enough affordable, nutritious food"Climate change is a growing threat to global food security."
genetically modified (GM) cropsplants whose genes have been changed to improve them"Genetically modified crops can survive drought and resist disease."
intensive farmingproducing as much food as possible from the land using chemicals and machinery"Intensive farming keeps prices low but can damage the soil."
food milesthe distance food travels from where it is produced to where it is eaten"Buying local vegetables reduces food miles."
malnutritionpoor health caused by not eating enough, or not enough of the right food"Malnutrition still affects millions of children under five."
additivessubstances added to food to improve its colour, flavour or shelf life"Parents are often advised to avoid snacks that are full of additives."
sugar taxa tax on sugary drinks and snacks designed to reduce how much people consume"A sugar tax persuaded several drinks companies to change their recipes."
plant-based dieta way of eating that consists mainly or entirely of foods from plants"A plant-based diet has a much smaller environmental impact than one rich in meat."
food labellingthe information about ingredients and nutrition printed on packaging"Clear food labelling helps shoppers make healthier choices."
locally sourcedproduced near the place where it is sold or eaten"The restaurant uses only locally sourced ingredients."

Key Collocations

  • follow / maintain a balanced diet
  • cut down on sugar / salt
  • skip a meal
  • grab a quick bite
  • cook from scratch
  • tackle food waste

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "The growing popularity of fast food is closely linked to rising obesity rates."
  • "Clear labelling allows consumers to make informed choices about what they eat."
  • "Cooking lessons at school would give young people the skills to eat well on a small budget."
  • "Taxing unhealthy products is one way to steer consumers towards better options."
  • "Traditional cooking is part of a country's cultural identity and deserves to be preserved."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. Wheat, rice and maize are the ________ of most of the world's population. (staple foods)
  2. Buying fruit that has been flown across the world adds greatly to its ________. (food miles)
  3. A ________ on fizzy drinks has encouraged manufacturers to reduce the sugar they contain. (sugar tax)
  4. Supermarkets could reduce ________ by donating unsold items to charities. (food waste)
  5. ________ keeps food cheap, but critics say it harms the soil and animal welfare. (Intensive farming)
  6. Even in wealthy countries, poor families can suffer from ________ because healthy food is expensive. (malnutrition)
  7. People who work long hours often rely on ________ that only needs heating up. (convenience food)
  8. Better ________ would show shoppers exactly how much salt and sugar a product contains. (food labelling)
  9. Switching to a ________ is one of the most effective ways for an individual to reduce their environmental impact. (plant-based diet)
  10. Droughts and conflict are the greatest threats to ________ in the region. (food security)
