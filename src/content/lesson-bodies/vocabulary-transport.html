Topic Vocabulary

Transport & Traffic

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
traffic congestionroads so full of vehicles that traffic moves slowly or stops"Traffic congestion costs the economy billions in lost working time."
rush hourthe busiest time of day on roads and public transport, when people travel to and from work"The journey takes twice as long during rush hour."
private vehiclea car or motorbike owned by an individual rather than a company or the state"Too many commuters still depend on private vehicles."
cycle lanea part of the road reserved for bicycles"Protected cycle lanes encourage more people to ride to work."
pedestrian zonean area of a town where vehicles are not allowed"Turning the main square into a pedestrian zone brought shoppers back."
road safetymeasures and behaviour that prevent accidents on the road"Lower speed limits near schools improve road safety."
faresthe prices passengers pay to travel by bus, train, taxi or plane"Rising fares are pushing passengers back into their cars."
car ownershipthe proportion of people who own a car"Car ownership has tripled in the country since 2000."
rail networkthe system of railway lines that connects places"Extending the rail network would take lorries off the roads."
exhaust fumesthe harmful gases that come out of a vehicle's engine"Exhaust fumes are a major cause of breathing problems in cities."

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
congestion chargea fee that drivers pay to enter a busy city centre"London's congestion charge cut city-centre traffic noticeably."
electric vehiclea car or bus powered by a battery rather than petrol or diesel"Sales of electric vehicles are rising as batteries become cheaper."
car-sharing schemea system in which several people use the same car instead of each owning one"A car-sharing scheme reduces the number of vehicles parked on each street."
high-speed railtrains that travel much faster than ordinary services"High-speed rail can compete with flying on journeys of a few hundred kilometres."
park-and-ridea scheme where drivers leave their cars on the edge of town and continue by bus"The park-and-ride service keeps thousands of cars out of the historic centre."
emissions standardslegal limits on the pollution that vehicles may produce"Stricter emissions standards have forced manufacturers to build cleaner engines."
toll roada road that drivers must pay to use"The new motorway is a toll road paid for by private investors."
freightgoods that are carried by lorry, train, ship or plane"Moving freight by rail rather than road would cut emissions."
transport linksthe road, rail and air connections between places"Good transport links attract businesses to a region."
low-emission zonean area that the most polluting vehicles must pay to enter or may not enter at all"The low-emission zone has improved air quality around the city's schools."

Key Collocations

  • ease / relieve traffic congestion
  • invest in public transport
  • commute by train / by car
  • be stuck in a traffic jam
  • subsidise fares
  • reduce reliance on private cars

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "Investing in reliable public transport is the most effective way to reduce congestion."
  • "Unless alternatives are affordable, drivers are unlikely to leave their cars at home."
  • "Building more roads tends to attract more traffic rather than relieve it."
  • "Measures such as a congestion charge discourage unnecessary journeys."
  • "Cheaper fares would make buses and trains a realistic choice for low-income commuters."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. Drivers entering the city centre on weekdays must pay a ________. (congestion charge)
  2. ________ from diesel engines are linked to asthma in children. (Exhaust fumes)
  3. Safe, separated ________ would persuade more commuters to travel by bicycle. (cycle lanes)
  4. A ________ lets drivers leave their cars outside the city and finish the journey by bus. (park-and-ride)
  5. Trains and buses are most crowded during ________. (rush hour)
  6. Carrying ________ by train instead of lorry would reduce both pollution and accidents. (freight)
  7. Remote regions struggle to attract employers because of poor ________. (transport links)
  8. When ________ rise faster than wages, passengers return to their cars. (fares)
  9. Closing the old town to cars and creating a ________ made it far more pleasant for shoppers. (pedestrian zone)
  10. Governments are offering grants to encourage people to buy an ________. (electric vehicle)
