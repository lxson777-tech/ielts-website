Topic Vocabulary

Arts & Culture

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
visual artsart forms that are looked at, such as painting, sculpture and photography"The city is famous for its visual arts scene."
performing artsart forms performed in front of an audience, such as theatre, dance and music"She studied performing arts before becoming an actor."
exhibitiona public display of works of art or other interesting objects"The exhibition attracted over a million visitors."
masterpiecea work of art of outstanding skill and quality"The painting is considered the artist's masterpiece."
contemporary artart produced by artists who are living and working today"Contemporary art often divides opinion."
creativitythe ability to produce original ideas or things"Art lessons develop children's creativity."
live performancea show that is watched in person as it happens, not recorded"Nothing compares with the atmosphere of a live performance."
arts fundingmoney given to support museums, theatres, orchestras and artists"Arts funding is often the first thing to be cut in a recession."
traditional craftsskills such as weaving or pottery that have been passed down through generations"Traditional crafts are disappearing as young people move to the cities."
cultural eventan organised activity such as a festival, concert or exhibition"The town holds a major cultural event every summer."

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
aestheticconnected with beauty and the way something looks"The building has great aesthetic appeal."
artistic expressioncommunicating ideas and feelings through art"Street murals are a powerful form of artistic expression."
patronagefinancial support given to artists by wealthy people or organisations"Many Renaissance painters depended on the patronage of rich families."
public artsculptures, murals and other art placed in streets, parks and squares"Public art can give a run-down area a sense of identity."
thought-provokingmaking people think seriously about a subject"It was a thought-provoking play about migration."
cultural enrichmentimprovement of people's lives through contact with art and ideas"Free museums offer cultural enrichment to everyone, whatever their income."
commercialisationorganising something mainly in order to make a profit"The commercialisation of music festivals has driven up ticket prices."
abstract artart that uses shapes and colours instead of showing real people or objects"Some visitors find abstract art difficult to understand."
creative industriesbusinesses based on creativity, such as film, design, fashion and publishing"The creative industries employ millions of people."
national identitythe shared sense of belonging to a particular nation"Traditional music is an important part of national identity."

Key Collocations

  • fund / subsidise the arts
  • appreciate art
  • stage a play / an exhibition
  • preserve traditional crafts
  • nurture creativity
  • appeal to a wide audience

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "Some people see the arts as a luxury, while others consider them essential to a civilised society."
  • "Government funding allows museums and galleries to remain open to everyone."
  • "Art and music develop qualities that academic subjects tend to neglect."
  • "Public money, critics argue, would be better spent on hospitals and schools."
  • "A country's art is one of the clearest records of its history and values."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. When budgets are tight, ________ is frequently cut before anything else. (arts funding)
  2. Skills such as weaving and pottery are ________ that may vanish within a generation. (traditional crafts)
  3. The ________ of major festivals means tickets are now too expensive for many fans. (commercialisation)
  4. Watching a ________ in a theatre is a completely different experience from watching a recording. (live performance)
  5. Statues and murals are forms of ________ that everyone can enjoy without paying. (public art)
  6. The ________, including film and fashion, make a large contribution to the economy. (creative industries)
  7. Music and drama lessons encourage ________ in a way that tests cannot. (creativity)
  8. The museum's new ________ of photography runs until the end of the year. (exhibition)
  9. Historically, great painters relied on the ________ of wealthy families. (patronage)
  10. The documentary was ________ and changed how many viewers saw the issue. (thought-provoking)
