About the Test

IELTS Writing at a Glance

The Basics

  • Total time: 60 minutes for two tasks: you manage the split yourself
  • Recommended: 20 minutes on Task 1, 40 minutes on Task 2
  • Minimum lengths: 150 words (Task 1) and 250 words (Task 2). Under-length answers are penalised
  • Task 2 is worth twice as much as Task 1. Never let it run out of time
  • No choice of question. You must answer what you are given
  • Answers must be written as one connected piece of text. Notes and bullet points are penalised
  • An answer that does not relate to the topic is penalised, however good the English is

The Two Tasks

  • Task 1: a report describing a chart, graph, table, map, process or diagram. Facts only: the task asks you to summarise what the visual shows, not what you think of it.
  • Task 2: a formal essay responding to an opinion, discussion, problem/solution or two-part question.
  • Task 2 topics are general interest. No specialist knowledge needed; you are marked on your English, not your ideas.

How Examiners Mark It

Each task is scored on four criteria, weighted equally (25% each). The examiner awards a whole band (0-9) per criterion, and the task score is their average.

CriterionWhat it measures
Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2)Did you fully answer the task. All parts, right format, developed ideas (and an overview in a Task 1 report)?
Coherence & CohesionLogical paragraphing, clear progression, and natural linking. Not mechanical connectors.
Lexical ResourceRange and precision of vocabulary, collocation, and spelling.
Grammatical Range & AccuracyVariety of sentence structures and how error-free they are.

Your overall Writing band = (Task 1 + Task 2 × 2) ÷ 3, rounded to the nearest half band. Our Writing Trainer scores you on exactly these criteria, using the official public band descriptors.

What this means for your target Because Task 2 counts double, your essay moves your Writing band twice as far as your report does. Master the essay first, then polish the report. And watch the clock: the single most common writing disaster is a brilliant Task 1 followed by a rushed, unfinished Task 2.
