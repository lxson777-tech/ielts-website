Plan, Map & Diagram Labelling

What is it?

  • The official name is Plan, Map or Diagram Labelling
  • The instruction line reads something like: "Label the map below. Write the correct letter, A-H, next to questions 11-15." Some labelling tasks instead say "Label the diagram below. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for each answer."
  • You are given a picture (a building plan, a street map, an outdoor site, a set of pictures, or a diagram of an object) and you label the numbered features on it
  • Answer format: usually a single letter chosen from a list printed on the question paper, but sometimes a word or words taken from the recording within a word limit. The instruction line tells you which
  • Appears most often in Part 2. A diagram of an object or process can also turn up in Parts 3 and 4

Key Rules

  • Always find the compass or the "you are here" starting point first. Every direction the speaker gives is relative to that point
  • The speaker usually moves through the map in one continuous direction, though they can double back, so keep listening rather than assuming
  • Answers follow the order you hear them, which usually matches the order you move around the map, not the numerical order of the letters
  • When you choose from a lettered list there are usually more letters than questions, so some are never used, exactly as with matching

How to Approach It

  1. In the pause before the recording starts, find north (or the entrance) on the map, and physically trace a finger from that point.
  2. Read the list of numbered features and picture what each one is, so you recognise it instantly when it is mentioned.
  3. As the speaker moves through the space, follow with your finger in real time, updating your position at every piece of directional language.
  4. The moment a feature is named, place your finger where it should be relative to your current position and lock in the nearest letter.
  5. If you lose your place, do not go back. Jump ahead to the next landmark word you recognise and pick up the route from there.
⚠ Trap: losing track of your position after missing one direction, so every answer after that is wrong too ⚠ Trap: confusing left/right with compass directions when the speaker switches between them ⚠ Trap: two features are close together on the map, and only the preposition (before, past, opposite) tells them apart
