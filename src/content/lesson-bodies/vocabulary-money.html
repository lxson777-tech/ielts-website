Topic Vocabulary

Money & Consumerism

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
disposable incomethe money a person has left to spend after paying taxes and essential bills"Young professionals living with their parents tend to have a high disposable income."
savingsmoney that a person keeps, usually in a bank, instead of spending"Many households have no savings to fall back on in an emergency."
debtmoney that is owed to a person, bank or company"Easy credit has left many young people in debt."
household budgeta plan of how a family's income will be spent"Rising energy bills are putting pressure on the household budget."
luxury goodsexpensive items that are pleasant to own but not necessary"Demand for luxury goods is growing fastest in Asia."
consumer spendingthe total amount that ordinary people spend on goods and services"Consumer spending usually falls when people fear losing their jobs."
bargainsomething bought for less than its usual price"Shoppers queue overnight to find a bargain in the sales."
online shoppingbuying goods over the internet"Online shopping has changed the character of the high street."
brand loyaltythe habit of buying the same brand again and again"Brand loyalty means that customers rarely compare prices."
value for moneygood quality in relation to the price paid"Shoppers are increasingly looking for value for money rather than famous labels."

Ten more words for the same topic. Learn these once the table above feels comfortable.

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
financial literacythe knowledge needed to manage money, debt and savings sensibly"Teaching financial literacy at school would help young adults avoid debt."
impulse buyingbuying things suddenly, without planning or thinking carefully"Shops place sweets by the till to encourage impulse buying."
materialismthe belief that money and possessions matter more than anything else"Critics say advertising encourages materialism in children."
throwaway culturea society in which goods are used briefly and then discarded"Cheap clothing has helped to create a throwaway culture."
cashless societya society in which people pay electronically rather than with notes and coins"A cashless society could exclude elderly people who do not bank online."
purchasing powerthe amount of goods and services that a sum of money can buy"Inflation reduces the purchasing power of wages."
interest ratethe percentage charged for borrowing money, or paid on savings"A rise in the interest rate makes loans more expensive."
living beyond your meansspending more money than you earn"Credit cards make living beyond your means dangerously easy."
consumer rightsthe legal protection that buyers have against unfair or unsafe products"Consumer rights allow shoppers to return faulty goods."
ethical consumptionchoosing products that do the least harm to people, animals and the planet"Ethical consumption is growing among younger shoppers."

Key Collocations

  • make ends meet
  • get into / pay off debt
  • shop around for the best price
  • save up for something
  • spend money on non-essentials
  • tighten your belt

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "In consumer societies, people are often judged by what they own rather than who they are."
  • "Easy access to credit encourages people to spend money they do not have."
  • "Teaching children to manage money would prepare them for the realities of adult life."
  • "The pursuit of wealth does not necessarily lead to greater happiness."
  • "Shopping has become a leisure activity in its own right rather than a way of meeting needs."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. After rent and bills, many young adults have very little ________ left. (disposable income)
  2. Placing chocolate next to the checkout is designed to encourage ________. (impulse buying)
  3. In a ________, people who do not use bank cards or smartphones may be left behind. (cashless society)
  4. When prices rise faster than wages, the ________ of ordinary workers falls. (purchasing power)
  5. Clothes that are worn twice and then binned are a symbol of our ________. (throwaway culture)
  6. Lessons in ________ would teach teenagers how loans and interest really work. (financial literacy)
  7. Customers with strong ________ keep buying the same phone even when rivals are cheaper. (brand loyalty)
  8. Thanks to ________, shoppers can demand a refund if a product turns out to be faulty. (consumer rights)
  9. People who rely on credit cards to fund holidays are ________. (living beyond their means)
  10. A higher ________ rewards savers but makes borrowing more expensive. (interest rate)
