Sentence Completion

What is it?

  • The official name is Sentence Completion
  • The instruction line reads something like: "Complete the sentences below. Write NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS for each answer."
  • You are given a sentence with one gap, summarising a point from the recording, and you complete it with the missing word or short phrase
  • Answer format: the exact word(s) you hear, within the stated limit
  • Appears most often in Part 4, and sometimes in Part 3

Key Rules

  • The completed sentence must be grammatically correct. This alone rules out many wrong guesses before you even check the meaning
  • Words come directly from the recording, not a paraphrase you invent yourself
  • Answers follow the order of the sentences on the page
  • Spelling and word limit are both marked strictly, exactly as in every other completion task
  • A number counts as one word and a hyphenated word counts as one word, so "well-known" fits a one-word limit

How to Approach It

  1. In the pause before the recording, read each sentence and decide what part of speech the gap needs: a noun, a verb, an adjective, a number.
  2. Use that same pause to predict roughly what the missing idea might be, based on the rest of the sentence.
  3. Listen in the order of the sentences, since the lecture or talk moves through the same points in the same sequence.
  4. When you hear content that matches the sentence's meaning, check silently that the exact word you plan to write completes the sentence grammatically.
  5. Write the word you actually heard, not a synonym of it, and move on the instant you have it.
⚠ Trap: the word you hear does not fit the sentence grammatically unless you change its form slightly, so listen for the exact form used ⚠ Trap: a tempting word appears just before the true answer, as a distractor ⚠ Trap: writing a synonym instead of the word actually spoken
Grammar is your best clue Before you even think about meaning, look at what comes immediately before and after the gap. If the gap follows "the", you almost certainly need a noun. If it follows "to", you likely need a verb. This narrows the field of possible answers dramatically, often before you have heard a single word of the recording.

Writing your answer

More marks are lost on the form of an answer than on understanding the recording. You hear the right idea, you know it is the right idea, and then you write it down in a way that cannot be accepted. Two habits fix nearly all of it: predict the word type in the pause before you listen, then follow the answer rules when you write.

Half 1. Predict the word type before you listen

The note above says grammar is your best clue. Here is how to turn that into a checklist you run on every sentence during the pause.

  • "a" or "the" in front of the gap means the answer is a noun. "A" tells you even more: the noun must be countable and singular, so an uncountable word such as "training", "equipment", "research" or "progress" is impossible there, however clearly you hear it.
  • A preposition next to the gap tells you the shape of the answer. "...depends on ___" needs a thing or an -ing form. "...was replaced by a ___" needs a thing. When the gap comes first and a preposition follows it ("The ___ of the sample was measured"), you need a noun again.
  • Read the finished sentence back to yourself with your answer inside it, in your head, before the next sentence comes. If it does not sound like English, it is wrong, however clearly you heard the words.

Worked example: grammar alone decides it

Recording: "All volunteers do safety training before they travel, and each one also fills in a short health form."

Sentence 34 (NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS): "Before the trip, every volunteer must complete a ___."

✗ Looks right, marked wrong
safety training

By meaning it is tempting, because it is something every volunteer completes before travelling, and the speaker says it first. But "training" is uncountable, and "a safety training" is not English. The "a" rules it out on its own.

✓ Correct
short health form

A countable singular noun with its describing words, so it fits after "a", it is exactly what the speaker said, and it is three words.

Half 2. The answer rules

Rule Marked wrong Marked right
Write the words exactly as you hear them. Never tidy them into wording of your own.
Recording: "the main obstacle was the cost of transport"		 price of travel cost of transport
Keep singular or plural as you hear it. Do not change a word's number to make your sentence read more smoothly.
Recording: "the students kept detailed diaries"		 diary diaries
Write only the missing words. If the sentence already prints "the" or "and", do not write it again.
Sentence: "The scheme is funded by the ___ and local businesses."
Recording: "funding comes from the city council and from local businesses"		 city council and local businesses city council
Keep a word that carries meaning, even when it makes the answer longer.
Recording: "the biggest barrier was low self-confidence"		 self-confidence low self-confidence
Drop a pronoun or a comparative the sentence already contains.
Sentence: "Modern turbines are ___ than those built in the 1990s."
Recording: "modern turbines are far quieter than those built in the nineties"		 quieter than those far quieter
Include a preposition when the phrase needs one.
Sentence: "The diagrams help students ___ the raw data."
Recording: "the diagrams help students make sense of the raw data"		 make sense make sense of
Count the words against the limit. A hyphenated word counts as one word, and a number written in digits counts as one.
Limit: NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER
Recording: "the team used twelve purpose-built tanks"		 twelve tanks that were purpose-built 12 purpose-built tanks
Write one answer only. In the real test you never write two options, never use a slash, never use brackets. far quieter / much quieter far quieter
Spelling and plurals are marked, and you write what you hear. When a name or a term is spelled out letter by letter, copy it letter for letter.
Recording: "named after the geologist Wegener, W-E-G-E-N-E-R"		 Vegener Wegener
