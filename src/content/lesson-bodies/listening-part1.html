Part 1 · Easiest

Everyday Conversation

Most common question types here: form, note or table completion and short-answer questions.

What is it?

  • Conversation between two speakers in an everyday social context, usually transactional
  • Examples: booking a hotel, registering at a sports club, enquiring about a course
  • Typical question type: form / note completion
  • The answers are mostly names, numbers, addresses, dates and times

Why People Still Lose Marks Here

  • Spelling: names are spelled out letter by letter; write as you hear
  • -teen vs -ty: fourteen (14) sounds like "forTEEN"; forty (40) sounds like "FORty"
  • Corrections: when you hear "actually, make that Thursday," the corrected version is the answer
  • Word limits: an instruction such as "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER" is enforced strictly. A number counts as one word, and a hyphenated word such as "check-in" counts as one word

Strategy

  1. In the preview time, read the form and predict the type of each answer: a name? a number? a date?
  2. Follow the questions in order. Answers come in sequence.
  3. Write as you listen; if you miss one, move on immediately.
  4. Dates can be written with figures and words: "the fifth of March" can be written 5 March or 5th March. Avoid all-figure forms such as 5/3, because day-first and month-first conventions differ.
Spelling counts Unlike Reading, a misspelled answer in Listening is marked wrong. Double-check proper nouns, numbers and addresses during the transfer time (on the paper-based test you get 10 minutes to transfer answers to the answer sheet at the end; on the computer-delivered test there is no separate transfer stage, just a 2-minute check at the end of the test to review your on-screen answers).
