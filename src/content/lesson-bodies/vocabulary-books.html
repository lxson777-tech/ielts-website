Topic Vocabulary

Books & Reading

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
fiction / non-fictionwriting about imagined events, and writing about real facts"I read fiction for pleasure and non-fiction to learn something."
novela long written story about imaginary people and events"She published her first novel at the age of thirty."
authorthe person who wrote a book"The author spent six years researching the subject."
e-booka book that is read on a screen rather than on paper"E-books are convenient for travelling but harder to lend."
audiobooka recording of a book being read aloud"Audiobooks let people read during their commute."
public librarya building where anyone can borrow books free of charge"Public libraries are one of the few free spaces left in a city."
literacythe ability to read and write"Adult literacy programmes changed thousands of lives."
reading habitthe practice of reading regularly"A reading habit formed in childhood usually lasts for life."
textbooka book used for studying a subject"Digital textbooks are cheaper and easier to update."
genre fictionnovels written to a popular type, such as crime, romance or science fiction"Genre fiction accounts for most of what libraries lend."

Word / PhraseMeaningExample in Context
literaryconnected with literature, especially writing of high artistic quality"The prize is awarded for literary rather than commercial success."
imaginationthe ability to form pictures and ideas in your mind"Reading develops a child's imagination in a way that screens do not."

skimto read quickly to get the general idea"I skim the news in the morning and read properly in the evening."
concentration spanhow long a person can keep their attention on one thing"Reading a long book strengthens the concentration span."
out of printno longer being produced by the publisher"The book has been out of print for twenty years."
publishing industrythe business of producing and selling books"The publishing industry has been reshaped by online retailers."
critically acclaimedpraised by professional reviewers"Her second novel was critically acclaimed but sold poorly."
reference booka book such as a dictionary that you consult rather than read through"Reference books have largely been replaced by websites."
browseto look through books or shelves without a fixed purpose"I like to browse in a bookshop with no particular title in mind."

Key Collocations

  • be well / widely read
  • borrow / return a book
  • curl up with a good book
  • get into a book
  • instil a love of reading
  • flick through the pages

Useful Essay Phrases

  • "Reading for pleasure is one of the strongest predictors of success at school."
  • "Libraries do far more than lend books: they provide warmth, internet access and a quiet place to study."
  • "Digital formats have made reading more convenient without making people read more."
  • "Children copy the habits they see at home, so parents who read tend to raise readers."
  • "Screens compete for exactly the attention that reading a book requires."
Show Practice Exercise

Fill in the gaps using words from this section:

  1. Wide reading is the most natural route to ________, because words are met in context. (vocabulary acquisition)
  2. Many people now listen to an ________ while driving or exercising. (audiobook)
  3. The novel was ________ but never became a bestseller. (critically acclaimed)
  4. A ________ offers free internet and a warm place to work, not only books. (public library)
  5. I prefer to ________ the shelves rather than search for one particular title. (browse)
  6. Reading a long novel gradually builds a child's ________. (concentration span)
  7. The title has been ________ for two decades and is now hard to find. (out of print)
  8. Improving adult ________ is one of the cheapest ways to reduce poverty. (literacy)
  9. I ________ the headlines in the morning and read the articles later. (skim)
  10. Crime and romance, the most borrowed categories, are both ________. (genre fiction)
