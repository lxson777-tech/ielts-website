Form, Note, Table & Flow-chart Completion

What is it?

  • The official name covers several formats: Form Completion, Note Completion, Table Completion and Flow-chart Completion, all testing the same skill
  • The instruction line reads something like: "Complete the form below. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer."
  • You fill numbered gaps in a form, a set of notes, a table, or a flow-chart showing a process, using words taken straight from the recording
  • Answer format: the exact word(s) and/or number you hear, within the stated limit
  • Forms and notes dominate Part 1; tables and flow-charts appear more in Parts 3 and 4

Key Rules

  • The word limit is enforced strictly: one word over the limit means the answer is marked wrong even if it is otherwise correct
  • A number counts as one word, and a hyphenated word such as "check-in" counts as one word. Contractions such as "don't" are never tested
  • Spelling must be correct, including names, which are usually spelled out letter by letter in the recording
  • Numbers, dates and reference codes must be copied exactly as heard
  • Answers always follow the order of the form, top to bottom, or the order of the flow-chart's steps

How to Approach It

  1. In the pause before the recording, read every heading and label on the form, and decide what type of answer each gap needs: a name, a number, an address, a date.
  2. Note the word limit before you start, and keep it in your head for every single gap, not just the tricky ones.
  3. Follow the form top to bottom as you listen, since the speakers move through it in that exact order.
  4. When a name or code is spelled aloud, write each letter as you hear it, then check it reads as a real word once the speaker finishes.
  5. If a detail is corrected partway through ("sorry, that should be...", "actually, let's make that..."), cross out your first answer and use the correction.
⚠ Trap: names are spelled out letter by letter, and one mis-heard letter fails the whole answer ⚠ Trap: numbers, dates and phone numbers are read in an unfamiliar order or grouping ⚠ Trap: going one word over the stated limit
Spelling still counts A form completion answer that is correct in substance but misspelled is marked wrong, exactly as in Part 1. Treat spelled-out names and codes as the moment in the recording that deserves your fullest concentration, not a moment to relax because the hard listening is "over".

Customer: Hi, I'd like to arrange an installation, please.
Agent: Of course. Can I take your full name?
Customer: It's Radoslav Petrov. That's R-A-D-O-S-L-A-V, and the surname is P-E-T-R-O-V.
Agent: Thank you. And a contact number, please?
Customer: It's oh-seven-nine-two-three, five-five-six-one-oh-two.
Agent: Got it. What's the property address?
Customer: Flat 4, 18 Maple Court.
Agent: And the postcode?
Customer: NW6 4DP.
Agent: Perfect. We currently have two appointment slots next week: Tuesday the ninth, or Thursday the eleventh.
Customer: Thursday would suit me much better.
Agent: Morning or afternoon?
Customer: Afternoon, please, around two if possible.
Agent: I can offer two o'clock on Thursday the eleventh. The engineer will need up to three hours, so do make sure someone's home until five.
Customer: That's fine, I'll be working from home that day anyway.
Agent: Wonderful. Your reference number is BR-4472. Keep that safe in case you need to reschedule.
Customer: Got it, thank you very much.
Agent: You're welcome, you'll receive a confirmation text within the hour.
