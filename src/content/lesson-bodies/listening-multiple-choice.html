Multiple Choice

What is it?

  • The official name on the exam is simply Multiple Choice
  • The instruction line reads something like: "Choose the correct letter, A, B or C.", or sometimes "Choose TWO letters, A-E."
  • Two shapes exist: a question followed by three options, or the first half of a sentence followed by three possible endings
  • You write a letter as your answer, never a word
  • Appears most often in Parts 2 and 3, occasionally in Part 4
  • The questions come in the order you hear them: question 2 is answered after question 1, never before

Key Rules

  • A "choose TWO" task fills two of the 40 question numbers and carries two marks, one for each correct letter, so one right letter still earns one mark
  • The two (or three) letters can be written in any order
  • The recording will usually mention every option at some point, so hearing a word from an option does not make it correct
  • Options are close in length and grammar on purpose, so surface similarity tells you nothing

How to Approach It

  1. In the pause before this part starts, read the question stems and all the options, and underline the key difference between them.
  2. Use that same pause to predict what kind of information will separate the right answer from the wrong ones: a reason, a number, a name, a time.
  3. Listen in order. As soon as you hear the topic of question one begin, focus fully on distinguishing the options rather than waiting to recognise the "right" words.
  4. When the speaker corrects themselves or adds "actually" or "on second thoughts", drop your first guess. The corrected statement is the one that counts.
  5. If you are unsure between two options, mark your best guess and move on immediately. A question you are still deciding on is a question that makes you miss the next one.
⚠ Trap: the speaker mentions all three options, then corrects themselves. Only the final version is correct ⚠ Trap: two options sound almost identical apart from one key word ⚠ Trap: the right answer is expressed using completely different words from the option itself
"Choose TWO letters" questions These take up two of the 40 question numbers and are marked as two separate points, one mark for each correct letter. The recording usually gives both correct pieces of information close together, but not always adjacent, so keep listening for both even after you think you have found one.
Transcript. Read at a steady pace, as if listening. (Simulation: Staff Induction, Parking Options)

Induction Leader: Before we finish today's tour, let me give you some practical information about parking, since this comes up every time. The staff car park on Elm Street is the closest to the building, but it's reserved for permit holders only, and permits take about a month to process, so don't count on using that space during your first few weeks. Some of you might think the visitor car park on Birch Avenue is a good alternative, since it's free for the first two hours. Actually, thinking about it, that one isn't ideal either, because meetings often run over two hours, and the hourly rate after that is fairly steep. What I'd genuinely recommend, at least until your permit for Elm Street comes through, is the multi-storey on Willow Road. It's a five-minute walk from the office, but it charges a flat daily rate that works out far cheaper than either of the other two if you're there for a full working day. Once your Elm Street permit is approved, you're welcome to switch over, but for now, Willow Road is your best option by some distance. One more thing: cycling is actively encouraged, and there are secure bike racks just inside the main entrance, with lockers and showers on the ground floor for anyone who cycles in.
